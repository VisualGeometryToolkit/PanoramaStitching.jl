Registration

PanoramaStitching.register_rotations — Function
register_rotations(frames, camera; projection = camera, raw_scale, circle, tone_percentile, detector_margin_px, asift_tilts, max_detections,
                   descriptor, matcher, min_score, pairs, max_lag, overlap_deg, min_inliers, seed,
                   scoring = nothing, local_optimization = nothing, max_trials, min_trials,
                   averaging_loss, bundle_loss, bundle_scale_multiple, bundle_iterations, bundle_max_iter, bundle_ftol,
                   bundle_xtol, bundle_whitening = :none, execution_provider = :cpu, say = _ -> nothing) -> Registration

The rotations of the pure-rotation sequence frames (H × W × 3 linear images in capture order, raw_scale camera pixels per frame pixel) seen by the calibrated camera (fixed; any camera with backproject_with_jacobian and backproject), projected in the bundle through projection, its closed-form forward map (the camera itself when it is a CameraModel; for a grid model its SplineProjection, see refine_rotations), with the view graph, the tracks and the bundle's statistics. Every setting is the caller's configuration, none defaulted but the RANSAC's:

  • features: circle (a Core Circle or Ellipse in camera pixels, WholePlane() for a lens with none), tone_percentile, detector_margin_px, asift_tilts (nothing: IMAS-25), max_detections — see detect_features; descriptor and matcher, VisualGeometryToolkitFeatures' LoMaDescriptor and LoMaMatcher variants (:b, :b128), one ONNX session each for the whole registration on execution_provider (:cpu, the default, or :cuda, which needs import CUDA, cuDNN and a functional device); min_score.
  • pairs: pairs = :all (every frame pair) or :lag_overlap (pairs at most max_lag apart; while the verified pairs leave the frames in more than one part, as when a lag pair is dropped, the pair between two parts nearest in capture order; then those whose optical axes, aimed by the rotation averaging of the verified pairs, are within overlap_deg); a pair is verified by find_rotation (its scoring and local_optimization objects, nothing for VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation's defaults; max_trials, min_trials; the sampler seeded from seed) and kept with at least min_inliers inliers.
  • graph and bundle: averaging_loss, bundle_loss (stack loss shapes), bundle_scale_multiple, bundle_iterations, bundle_max_iter, bundle_ftol, bundle_xtol — see refine_rotations. bundle_whitening = :none (the default) weighs every observation alike, in pixels; :keypoint_covariance whitens each by its keypoint's position covariance (FrameFeatures's pixel_covariances; a keypoint the detector did not refine takes the isotropic median), the loss acting on the whitened residuals.

say receives one line per frame, pair and step.

A task verb: it composes the estimators above and does none of its own. Each pair's RANSAC sampler is seeded from seed and the pair's frame indices, a stream of its own per (seed, i, j). A pairs other than :all or :lag_overlap, a max_lag below 1 with :lag_overlap, a bundle_whitening other than :none or :keypoint_covariance, an execution_provider other than :cpu or :cuda and a projection that is no closed-form forward map (a grid model without its SplineProjection) are an ArgumentError before any feature is detected. With either pairs, a view graph that is not connected once the pairs with fewer than min_inliers inliers are dropped (its frames have no common frame, ViewGraph) is an ArgumentError too.

PanoramaStitching.chain_tracks — Function
chain_tracks(matches) -> (tracks, n_dropped)

The tracks of a sequence: the connected sets of observations (frame, keypoint) through every pair's inlier matches, matches an AbstractDict keyed by frame pairs (i, j) whose value is a vector of keypoint index pairs (a, b) (keypoint a of frame i ↔ keypoint b of frame j). Union-find over the observations (DataStructures.jl's IntDisjointSets). A track that holds two different keypoints of one frame is inconsistent (a wrong match chained through the graph) and is DROPPED whole; their count is n_dropped. Every track returned has observations in at least two frames, sorted by frame then keypoint.

PanoramaStitching.refine_rotations — Function
refine_rotations(camera, rotations, tracks; projection = camera, loss, scale_multiple, iterations, max_iter, ftol, xtol,
                 eliminate = true) -> FitResult{Dict{K, RotMatrix{3, Float64}}, RotationBundleMetadata}

The rotation bundle of a pure-rotation sequence seen by the calibrated camera (fixed; its backproject and backproject_with_jacobian give the start and the Jacobian), projected through projection, the camera's closed-form forward map π: the camera itself when it is a CameraModel (its imaging_transform), or a SplineProjection — for a grid model, which has no closed-form forward map, its VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.fit_camera(SplineProjection, grid, region; tolerance) (an ArgumentError names it when missing). The rotations R_k (d = R_k·f_k) of every frame but the first of the sorted keys of rotations (the gauge, held fixed) and one direction d_j per track minimizing the robust sum of the pixel reprojection errors π(R_kᵀ d_j) − x_jk of every observation. A refine: it starts from rotations (a rotation averaging of the view graph, VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.fit_rotation_averaging) and each track's mean bearing in the common frame.

  • tracks — AbstractVector of tracks, each an AbstractVector of frame => pixel observations (pixels in the camera's frame), two frames at least. A pixel may be VisualGeometryToolkitCore's Uncertain(pixel, CovMat(Σ)), its 2 × 2 covariance Σ = LLᵀ (finite, positive definite, up to one global scale): the residual is then whitened, L⁻¹(π(R_kᵀ d_j) − x_jk), and the loss acts on the whitened residual. Every observation carries a covariance or none does.
  • loss, scale_multiple — the stack loss shape (NLLSsolver's robustifier, loss_robustifier) and its width as a multiple of the Rayleigh scale of the residual norms (pixels, or whitened: the one global scale that absorbs the noise level and the model error the covariances leave out) at the start of each of the iterations NLLSsolver runs (max_iter, ftol, xtol each: nlls_options).
  • eliminate — whether NLLSsolver may eliminate the directions (the Schur complement, where the loaded NLLSsolver has it); false solves every unknown together. Both reach the same optimum.

A refine ends in the local minimum its start leads to; the result's retcode and its metadata's outcome (RotationBundleMetadata) say how the bundle ended. A track observed in fewer than two frames, or naming a frame without a start rotation, some observations with a covariance and some without, and a covariance that is not finite and positive definite are an ArgumentError.

source
PanoramaStitching.RotationBundleMetadata — Type
RotationBundleMetadata

FitResult metadata of refine_rotations: directions (one unit direction per track, the common frame), residuals (per track, the pixel reprojection error norm of each of its observations at the result, unwhitened), loss_width (of the last NLLSsolver run: px, or in whitened units when the observations carry covariances), n_observations, start_nonfinite (the observations whose residual or Jacobian is not finite at the start: a start prediction outside the lens's domain, where the camera has no back-projection Jacobian) and outcome, the bundle's outcome:

  • :not_run — iterations = 0 (the start's rotations are the result);
  • :converged — the retcode is :converged;
  • :nonfinite_start — NLLSsolver stopped at its start point because its start is not finite: the retcode is :solve_failed, the rotations are the start's bit for bit (no step was tried) and start_nonfinite > 0. A rectilinear frame whose observations reach its corners, a start (the rotation averaging's) off by a fraction of a degree and a grid lens calibrated over the sensor at most give this (the Tamron 20 mm of 2026-10-29);
  • otherwise the retcode itself (:max_iterations, :stalled, :solve_failed, …).

source
PanoramaStitching.sample_frames — Function
sample_frames(camera, frames, rotations, directions; saturated, circle, raw_scale, rim_margin_px, fov_deg, vignetting,
              plane_offsets = nothing) -> FrameSamples

The frames sampled at directions (unit 3-vectors of the common frame, any array): for every frame k and direction d, the ray f = R_kᵀ·d (rotations[k] with d = R_k·f), its exact sub-pixel sensor point u = π(f) through the calibrated camera (a CameraModel through its imaging_transform, or a SplineProjection — a grid model's forward map, fit_camera(SplineProjection, grid, region; tolerance)), the frame's value there by bilinear sampling (Core's image_sample!, no smoothing) and the vignetting model evaluated continuously there, vignetting(u, f) (a model defined on the sensor reads u, one defined on the ray's direction reads f). These are the observations of a vignetting validation (I_k / V_k must agree across the frames that see a direction: their spread is the model's error) and of a panorama vignetting fit (I_k = L(d)·V(u_k)).

A frame sees a direction by render_panorama's masks: the ray within fov_deg of the camera frame's z axis, the sensor point inside circle (a Core Circle or Ellipse in camera pixels, or WholePlane() when the lens has no image circle) less rim_margin_px and at least 2 px inside the sensor, and no saturated pixel among those any plane's value blends (the four at each plane's own sample position). frames are H × W × 3 linear images with raw_scale camera pixels per frame pixel, saturated their H × W masks; plane_offsets the planes' sample positions relative to the pixel centre, frame pixels (nothing: all at the centre; a 2 × 2 Bayer binning's R and B at (±¼, ±¼), VisualGeometryToolkitFeatures' load_raw(…; mode = :bin2).plane_offsets): each plane is read at the sensor point less its offset.

Every step is a batch on the backend that owns frames and directions (Core's pointwise!, image_sample!: one kernel each on a GPU array, the same code on the host), so for device arrays vignetting must be kernel-lawful (isbits, no allocation, no throw); it is evaluated only where the frame sees the direction. The precision is the directions' element type.

PanoramaStitching.panorama_view — Function
panorama_view(projection, extent, deg_per_pixel; up = I) -> PanoramaView
panorama_view(camera, width, height; rotation = I) -> PanoramaView

The raster of a panorama and its pixel → ray map.

  • A cylindrical-family projection (Core's Equirectangular(), Cylindrical(), CylindricalEqualArea(), Mercator()): the raster spans extent = (lon₀, lon₁, lat₀, lat₁) degrees, deg_per_pixel along the longitude (its columns, from lon₀) and the same step in the projection's vertical coordinate (its rows, from lat₁ at the top: radians of latitude for the equirectangular, of tan φ, sin φ or asinh(tan φ) for the others), in the frame up (d = up·d_up, up_frame for a true-up panorama). Its pixel → ray map is LinearMap(up) ∘ inv(projection) ∘ raster, and its extent the raster's own (the latitudes of its row edges).
  • An azimuthal view, a virtual camera (a Core CameraModel): its width × height pixels, their rays LinearMap(rotation) ∘ Backprojection(camera) (d = rotation·f, f the virtual camera's ray). No new projection type: the lens chooses the view.
julia> using PanoramaStitching, StaticArrays

julia> using VisualGeometryToolkitCore: CameraModel, Lens, Perspective, Stereographic, Cylindrical, px

julia> cyl = panorama_view(Cylindrical(), (-120.0, 120.0, -40.0, 40.0), 0.05);   # cylindrical

julia> cyl.size                                     # rows in steps of tan φ, columns of longitude
(1923, 4800)

julia> rect = panorama_view(CameraModel(1000.0px, SA[960.0px, 540.0px]; lens = Lens(Perspective())), 1920, 1080);

julia> rect.size                                    # rectilinear
(1080, 1920)

julia> down = SMatrix{3, 3}(1.0, 0, 0, 0, 0, -1, 0, 1, 0);   # its axis along +y (down)

julia> planet = panorama_view(CameraModel(400.0px, SA[1000.0px, 1000.0px]; lens = Lens(Stereographic())), 2000, 2000;
                              rotation = down);    # a 'little planet'

julia> planet.size
(2000, 2000)

PanoramaStitching.render_panorama — Function
render_panorama(camera, frames, rotations, view; vignetting, circle, raw_scale, rim_margin_px, fov_deg,
                plane_offsets = nothing, gains = nothing) -> (; image, owner, coverage, extent)

The panorama of frames in the raster view (panorama_view) by the REVERSE warp, Core's image_warp/image_warp!: every pixel's ray d (the view's pixel → ray map) is rotated into each frame k (f = R_kᵀ·d, rotations[k] with d = R_k·f), projected through the calibrated camera to a sensor point between pixels (a CameraModel through its imaging_transform, a SplineProjection — a grid model's forward map, fit_camera(SplineProjection, grid, region; tolerance)), and sampled there bilinearly — one fused map camera ∘ R_kᵀ ∘ view.rays per frame — then divided by the vignetting vignetting(u, f), looked up at the sensor point u (camera pixels) and the ray f (the frame's camera frame) — a model defined on the sensor reads u, one defined on the ray's direction (a field angle) reads f — and by the frame's exposure gain gains[k] when gains is given (one per frame: fit_vignetting's metadata.gains; nothing, the frames taken as one exposure). No seam, no blend, and each pixel keeps the value of the LAST frame that sees it, so a wrong vignetting shows as a step at every frame boundary.

A frame sees a pixel when its ray is within fov_deg of the camera frame's z axis, its sensor point lies inside circle (a Core Circle or Ellipse in camera pixels, WholePlane() when the lens has no image circle) less rim_margin_px and at least 2 px inside the sensor — the geometric masks of sample_frames, evaluated as batches at the warp's own sensor points. The render shows the frames as they are: a saturated pixel is rendered with its clipped value (a window that clips in every frame comes out bright, not empty); saturation masks measurements only (sample_frames, fit_vignetting, score_seams). frames are H × W × 3 linear images with raw_scale camera pixels per frame pixel; plane_offsets are the three planes' sample positions relative to the pixel centre in frame pixels (nothing: all at the centre) — a 2 × 2 Bayer binning's R and B sit at (±¼, ±¼) (VisualGeometryToolkitFeatures' load_raw(…; mode = :bin2).plane_offsets), and each plane is read at the sensor point less its offset. The masks and the vignetting are at the pixel centre's point.

Every step runs on the backend that owns frames (GPU arrays: one kernel per warp, mask and update; the same code on the host); for device arrays vignetting must be kernel-lawful (it is evaluated only where a frame sees the pixel). The warp's math — the sensor points, the rays, the bilinear weights — is in Float64 whatever the frames' element type (a Float32 sensor coordinate of a 4000 px sensor is 2.4e-4 px coarse, enough to move a sampled value by 1e-4 relative); the image is in the frames' element type.

Returns image (rows × columns × 3, NaN where no frame sees the pixel), owner (the frame whose value a pixel holds, 0 for none), coverage (how many frames see each pixel), all on the frames' backend, and the view's extent.

The vignetting from the frames

PanoramaStitching.fit_vignetting — Function
fit_vignetting(space, camera, frames, rotations; saturated, circle, raw_scale, rim_margin_px, fov_deg, plane,
               plane_offsets, chart_spacing_deg, penalty_order, penalty_weight, vignetting_penalty_weight, frame_gains, solver)
    -> FitResult{SplineVignetting, VignettingMetadata}

The vignetting V of a camera that only rotated, from its frames alone (no flat field): every pixel of every frame is the scene's radiance in its direction times V at its sensor point, I_k(p) = L(R_k·f(p))·V(u(p)), and V is the factor the frames share where their views of the scene overlap. No interpolation of the frames: each value is read at its own pixel centre, its ray f(p) the camera's back-projection (a CameraModel or a CentralGenericModel, computed once for the pixel grid the frames share), and the scene L is a B-spline over an AzimuthalChart of the covered sphere whose values are fitted with V.

  • space: log V's spline, a RadialVignetting (a cubic B-spline in the distance from a centre) or a TensorVignetting (a cubic tensor B-spline over the sensor, no special point). A sweep about one axis cannot observe a free 2-D V's tilt across that axis (a field exp(α·f_y) is unchanged by a pure yaw, so L absorbs it): use the tensor space for frames with pitch and roll diversity.
  • The fit domain: every pixel of the H × W × 3 frames (raw_scale camera pixels per frame pixel) whose sensor point lies inside circle (a Core Circle or Ellipse in camera pixels, WholePlane() for a lens without one) less rim_margin_px and 2 px inside the sensor, with its ray within fov_deg of the optical axis. A row (a pixel of a frame) counts when its saturated mask is false and its value positive. plane is the plane fitted (2: green), read at its own sample position: plane_offsets (frame pixels, nothing for the pixel centre; VisualGeometryToolkitFeatures' load_raw(…).plane_offsets).
  • The model: log I = log L(d) + log V(u), least squares weighted by I² (normalized by its mean over the rows), the difference penalty of order penalty_order (2 or 3) and weight penalty_weight (in units of one row of mean weight) on L's controls along both chart axes, chart_spacing_deg apart, a weak second-difference penalty on V's controls of weight vignetting_penalty_weight relative to the V block's mean diagonal (it fixes the controls the domain does not reach), and the gauge mean log V = 0 over the domain's pixels.
  • The frames' exposure: with frame_gains = true, every frame has its own gain, log I_k = log g_k + log L(d) + log V(u), with K − 1 unknowns log g_k and the gauge g₁ = 1 (L carries the first frame's exposure). Use it when the exposure or the light can change between frames: a fixed shutter under a drifting sky changes every frame's level, and the fit then puts the difference into V — on the rooftop sweep the gains reached +18 % and V at the domain's edge came out 7 % too dark without them. With false every frame is taken at one exposure (g_k = 1). Required, no default.
  • The solve: solver, a ConjugateGradient (Jacobi-preconditioned, on the frames' backend) or a SparseCholesky (the same system assembled sparse and factored on the host).

Every stage runs on the backend that owns frames (GPU arrays: KernelAbstractions kernels and atomic scatter-add; host arrays, the same kernels on the CPU). The saturated masks live on the same backend.

Returns a FitResult whose estimate is the SplineVignetting (callable v(u, f), the vignetting keyword of render_panorama and sample_frames), retcode :converged or :max_iterations (CG) or :solved (Cholesky), iterations the CG's (0 for Cholesky), rms the I²-weighted rms of the log residual over n rows, and VignettingMetadata (its gains, the frames' g_k: the gains keyword of score_seams and render_panorama).

PanoramaStitching.AzimuthalChart — Type
AzimuthalChart

The chart of the scene's log L: the azimuthal-equidistant map of the sphere about e3 ((a, b) = θ·(cos φ, sin φ) degrees, θ the angle from e3, φ the azimuth from e1 towards e2), singular only at -e3, and a uniform cubic tensor B-spline of n × n controls spacing_deg apart over [−half_extent_deg, half_extent_deg]². fit_vignetting centres it so that its singular point is as far from the frames' coverage as the sphere allows (for a cap-like coverage, close to the mean optical axis; for a full 360° sweep, the pole the frames do not see).

PanoramaStitching.score_seams — Function
score_seams(camera, frames, rotations, view; vignetting, saturated, circle, raw_scale, rim_margin_px, fov_deg, plane,
            plane_offsets, chunk, gains = nothing) -> (; ratio, rms, mad, n)

How well vignetting makes the frames agree where they overlap, at every pixel of the panorama raster view (panorama_view): the pixel's ray is sampled in every frame (sample_frames: the exact sub-pixel sensor point, the frame's bilinear value of plane, the vignetting there, the masks — saturated pixels EXCLUDED, a clipped value is no measurement) and, where at least two frames measure it, ratio is the median over the other frames of log(I_k/V_k) − log(I_last/V_last), last the frame the unblended render_panorama shows (the highest index). A correct V leaves only noise, misregistration and parallax; a wrong one leaves a step at every frame boundary. The score is the ratio's spread over the pixels measured twice or more: rms and the robust mad (Core's mad_scale, the 1.4826·MAD; misalignment and moving content inflate the rms), over n pixels. gains (optional, one per frame: the exposure gains g_k of fit_vignetting's metadata.gains) divides each frame's exposure out as well, log(I_k/(g_k·V_k)); nothing takes the frames at one exposure.

The camera and the masks are sample_frames's (camera its forward map: a CameraModel or a grid's SplineProjection). The raster is sampled in chunks of chunk pixels (the samples are chunk × K × 3 values on the frames' backend: chunk to fit a shared GPU's memory). Returns ratio (rows × columns, NaN where fewer than two frames measure the pixel) on the frames' backend.
