PanoramaStitching.register_rotations — Function
register_rotations(frames, camera::BoundedCamera; frame_map, tone_percentile, detector_margin_px, asift_tilts, max_detections,
                   selection = :spread, detection_image = :tone, detection_noise = nothing, saturated = nothing,
                   descriptor, matcher, min_score, pairs, max_lag, overlap_deg, min_inliers, seed,
                   scoring = nothing, local_optimization_strategy = nothing, max_trials, min_trials,
                   averaging_optimization_strategy = <Estimation's>, cycle_tol = deg2rad(2), orientation_tol = deg2rad(5),
                   bundle_optimization_strategy = Triggs{WelschShape}(…), bundle_max_iter, bundle_ftol,
                   bundle_xtol, execution_provider = :auto, say = _ -> nothing) -> Registration
register_rotations(pairs::VerifiedPairs, camera::BoundedCamera; frame_map, averaging_optimization_strategy = <Estimation's>,
                   cycle_tol = deg2rad(2), orientation_tol = deg2rad(5), bundle_optimization_strategy = Triggs{WelschShape}(…),
                   bundle_max_iter, bundle_ftol, bundle_xtol, say = _ -> nothing) -> Registration

The rotations of the pure-rotation sequence frames (H × W × 3 linear images in capture order, frame_map their map onto the camera's pixels: VisualGeometryToolkitCore's raw_frame_map) seen by the calibrated camera (fixed; a VisualGeometryToolkitCore BoundedCamera, whose imaged_region bounds the features, projected in the bundle through its model's imaging_transform — a grid lens's SplineLens through its forward spline; see refine_rotations), with the view graph, the tracks and the bundle's statistics. Every setting is the caller's configuration, none defaulted but the RANSAC's:

  • features: inside the camera's imaged_region; tone_percentile, detector_margin_px, asift_tilts (a Features tilt_schedule name, :imas25 or :untilted, or its pairs), max_detections, selection, detection_image, detection_noise, saturated (the frames' clip masks, nothing: a LinearRaw's own, none for an array) — see detect_features (detection_image = :vst: the frames are LinearRaws); descriptor and matcher, VisualGeometryToolkitFeatures' LoMaDescriptor and LoMaMatcher variants (:b, :b128), one ONNX session each for the whole registration on ONNX Runtime's execution_provider (:auto, the default: CUDA where VisualGeometryToolkitFeatures finds a device and the environment holds CUDA_Runtime_jll and CUDNN_jll, else the CPU with a warning naming why; :cpu; :cuda, an error naming why where it cannot run), on Threads.nthreads() ONNX Runtime threads; min_score. The provider used is in the result's loma.
  • pairs: pairs = :all (every frame pair) or :lag_overlap (pairs at most max_lag apart; while the verified pairs leave the frames in more than one part, as when a lag pair is dropped, the pair between two parts nearest in capture order; then those whose optical axes, aimed by the rotation averaging of the verified pairs, are within overlap_deg); a pair is verified by find_rotation (its scoring and local_optimization_strategy objects, nothing for VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation's defaults; max_trials, min_trials; the sampler seeded from seed) and kept with at least min_inliers inliers. Its residuals are in the camera's pixels (pair_bearings: plain keypoints).
  • graph: VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation's fit_filtered_rotation_averaging of the verified pairs' view graph, its strategy averaging_optimization_strategy (an Irls{Shape}; by default VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation's [fit_rotation_averaging] optimization_strategy): the pairs off a cycle by more than cycle_tol removed (filter_cycles), the averaging, then the pairs off the averaged rotations by more than orientation_tol removed (filter_by_orientation) and the averaging again (radians; nothing skips that filter). A periodic scene verifies a pair one period off as well as the true one, and only the view graph tells them apart. The removed pairs are the result's removed; their matches make no track.
  • scale: per verified pair its RANSAC scorer's own scale ŝ (Core's noise_scale of its result: the consensus set's Student-t posterior scale) and the result's degrees of freedom, the pairs' scales (VerifiedPairs), px. No second scale estimate.
  • bundle: refine_rotations with bundle_optimization_strategy (a Triggs{Shape}, by default VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation's pixel bundle's) at scale the kept pairs' scales pooled by VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation's pooled_noise_scale (σ̂² = Σ νₚŝₚ² / Σ νₚ, νₚ the pair's degrees of freedom; the bundle records it as ScaleUpstream, its width the strategy's efficiency constant × σ̂), σ̂ the result's bundle_sigma; ONE LM run (bundle_max_iter, bundle_ftol, bundle_xtol: refine_rotations), the width fixed. No kept pair with a scale is an ArgumentError. Every observation is its keypoint's centre in the camera's pixels (through the frames' frame_map), the residuals in pixels. The bundle holds the camera's calibration (refine_rotations); the retired bundle_camera_free, bundle_scale_multiple, the whitening switch and local_optimization are an ArgumentError naming their successors (the autocalibration of a rotating camera is refine_camera_and_rotations, on the result's rotations and tracks).

say receives one line per frame, pair and step.

The second form starts from the verified pairs (verify_pairs, the first form's first step: everything up to the view graph) and their frames' frame_map, so a caller that keeps them reruns the averaging and the bundle alone; the first form is register_rotations(verify_pairs(frames, camera; frame_map, …), camera; frame_map, …).

A task verb: it composes the estimators above and does none of its own. Each pair's RANSAC sampler is seeded from seed and the pair's frame indices, a stream of its own per (seed, i, j). A pairs other than :all or :lag_overlap, a max_lag below 1 with :lag_overlap and an execution_provider other than :auto, :cpu or :cuda are an ArgumentError before any feature is detected. With either pairs, a view graph that is not connected once the pairs with fewer than min_inliers inliers are dropped (its frames have no common frame; VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation's require_connected) is an ArgumentError too.

PanoramaStitching.chain_tracks — Function
chain_tracks(matches) -> (tracks, n_dropped)

The tracks of a sequence: the connected sets of observations (frame, keypoint) through every pair's inlier matches, matches an AbstractDict keyed by frame pairs (i, j) whose value is a vector of keypoint index pairs (a, b) (keypoint a of frame i ↔ keypoint b of frame j). The connected components of the match graph, VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation's ViewGraph on the observations (components). PanoramaStitching.refine_rotations — Function
refine_rotations(camera, rotations, tracks; optimization_strategy = Triggs{WelschShape}(…), scale | width, max_iter,
                 ftol, xtol, eliminate = true) -> FitResult{Dict{K, RotMatrix{3, Float64}}, RotationBundleMetadata}

The rotation bundle of a pure-rotation sequence seen by the calibrated camera (fixed: the calibration is held, a rotation-only bundle with no parameter keyword — refine_camera_and_rotations is the autocalibration; its backproject and backproject_with_jacobian give the start and the Jacobian; a CameraModel or a BoundedCamera), projected through its closed-form forward map π, its model's imaging_transform (a grid lens's SplineLens through its forward spline: give a SplineLens camera one with VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation's fit_forward_spline(camera; tolerance), which keeps its backward, K and frame, while fit_camera(SplineLens, …) turns a gauge-K camera onto its optical axis, a new frame). A residual is NaN off the camera model's valid_domain (a grid lens's lattice), where its back-projection Jacobian is undefined; the lens coverage does not enter the bundle. The rotations R_k (d = R_k·f_k) of every frame but the first of the sorted keys of rotations (the gauge, held fixed) and one direction d_j per track minimizing the robust sum of the pixel reprojection errors π(R_kᵀ d_j) − x_jk of every observation. A refine: it starts from rotations (a rotation averaging of the view graph, VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.fit_rotation_averaging) and each track's mean bearing in the common frame.

An observation whose start prediction is not imaged (its residual or Jacobian not finite at the start: the prediction off the model's valid domain) is dropped, a track left in fewer than two frames is dropped whole (it keeps its start direction, held), and a partly kept track restarts from the mean bearing of the observations it keeps, its observations tested again until none is dropped: the solve is that of the kept observations alone. A frame other than the gauge left with no kept observation is held at its start rotation. The result's n is the number of observations kept; its metadata's n_observations the number given and n_dropped_start their difference (RotationBundleMetadata).

  • tracks — AbstractVector of tracks, each an AbstractVector of frame => pixel observations (pixels in the camera's frame), two frames at least. A pixel may be VisualGeometryToolkitCore's Uncertain(pixel, CovMat(Σ)), its 2 × 2 covariance Σ = LLᵀ (finite, positive definite, up to one global scale): the residual is then whitened, L⁻¹(π(R_kᵀ d_j) − x_jk), and the loss acts on the whitened residual. Every observation carries a covariance or none does.
  • optimization_strategy — the robust strategy of the robust-estimation contract: a Triggs{K} (VisualGeometryToolkitCore's; the default VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation's pixel bundle's, configs/default.toml [refine_camera_and_poses]: Triggs{WelschShape} at the shape's efficiency constant for a 2-D residual), its loss K as NLLSsolver's robustifier (loss_robustifier) at ONE width fixed for the bundle's one NLLSsolver run; or nothing, the plain least squares (no scale or width).
  • exactly one of scale = σ (the observations' noise scale, pixels or whitened units, or an upstream robust result: register_rotations hands the verified pairs' RANSAC scales pooled by Estimation's pooled_noise_scale) — the width is the strategy's efficiency constant × σ — or width = w (the loss width). Neither, or both, is an ArgumentError. max_iter, ftol (the relative cost decrease) and xtol (the largest step in chart units: radians for a rotation or a direction) are its stops (nlls_options); the result's iterations the LM iterations it took.
  • eliminate — whether NLLSsolver may eliminate the directions (the Schur complement, where the loaded NLLSsolver has it); false solves every unknown together. Both reach the same optimum.

A refine ends in the local minimum its start leads to; the result's termination record (Core's retcode and stop_rule: a ReturnCode.T and a StopRule; converged(result) its success) says how the bundle ended: NLLSsolver's stop, a step-size or rejection stop Success only where the robust cost is stationary at the result (Core's is_stationary at Estimation's [chart_lm] stationarity_tol), Stalled otherwise; max_iter = 0 (no solve: the start's rotations are the result) MaxIters with IterationCap. A track observed in fewer than two frames, or naming a frame without a start rotation, some observations with a covariance and some without, a covariance that is not finite and positive definite, and a start that keeps no observation (every track left in fewer than two imaged frames) are an ArgumentError, and so are the retired loss/loss_width (the strategy and its scale input replace them) and camera_free (the bundle holds the calibration: refine_camera_and_rotations frees it).

PanoramaStitching.refine_camera_and_rotations — Function
refine_camera_and_rotations(camera, rotations, tracks; optimization_strategy = Triggs{WelschShape}(…), scale | width,
                            fixed = (), max_iter, ftol, xtol, eliminate = true)
    -> FitResult{Dict{K, RotMatrix{3, Float64}}, RotationBundleMetadata}

The AUTOCALIBRATION of a rotating camera: no target, a pure-rotation sequence (Hartley 1994; Agapito, Hayman and Reid 2001). The rotation bundle of refine_rotations — the same observations, start, kept set, gauge and result — with the camera's free parameters one more variable: VisualGeometryToolkitCore's ParameterManifold of the camera's model restricted to them (the camera block of Estimation's refine_camera_and_poses), its Jacobian ForwardDiff through the chart. The only gauge is the first frame's rotation, held exactly; with it a rotating sequence leaves nothing else free, which the metadata's calibration shows (the reduced information, and the parameters' covariance: RotationBundleMetadata). The observations kept at the start are those the GIVEN camera images; the residuals, the loss width and the result are the refined camera's.

A contract refine:

  • optimization_strategy — the robust strategy, a Triggs{K} (VisualGeometryToolkitCore's; the default VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation's pixel bundle's, configs/default.toml [refine_camera_and_poses]: Triggs{WelschShape} at the shape's efficiency constant for a 2-D residual): the loss K as NLLSsolver's robustifier at ONE fixed width.
  • exactly one of scale = σ (the observations' noise scale, px or whitened units; or an upstream robust result) — the width is then the strategy's efficiency constant × σ — or width = w (the loss width itself).
  • fixed — the camera parameters HELD, by name or by a coefficient family's group (:k for k1…, :d, :p, :sx, :sy): VisualGeometryToolkitCore's free_parameter_names(model, fixed). The default () frees every parameter but the skew, which the stack holds by construction (named in fixed it is a no-op). An unknown or repeated entry, a camera with nothing free, a SplineLens camera (its controls have no static chart), and the retired camera_free, free, fix, fixed_params and hold are an ArgumentError.
  • max_iter, ftol, xtol, eliminate — as refine_rotations's.

PanoramaStitching.RotationBundleMetadata — Type
RotationBundleMetadata

FitResult metadata of refine_rotations and refine_camera_and_rotations: directions (one unit direction per track, the common frame), residuals (per track, the pixel reprojection error norm of each of its observations at the result, unwhitened), loss_width (the width the strategy ran at: px, or in whitened units when the observations carry covariances; NaN for the plain least squares), scale and scale_source (the scale record of the robust-estimation contract: the given scale, or NaN with ScaleNone for a width or the least squares; read through VisualGeometryToolkitCore's noise_scale/scale_source), n_observations (given), n_dropped_start (the observations left out of the solve: those whose residual or Jacobian is not finite at the start — a start prediction off the camera model's valid domain, where it has no projection and no back-projection Jacobian, as for a grid lens calibrated over the sensor whose frames' observations reach its corners from a start a fraction of a degree off, the Tamron 20 mm of 2026-10-29 — and the rest of a track left in fewer than two frames; a dropped observation's residual is NaN, and a track left out keeps its start direction), frames_unconstrained (the frames other than the gauge left with no kept observation: each is held at its start rotation),

camera (the camera at the result: the one given by refine_rotations, which holds it, else the refined one, of the given camera's type and, for a BoundedCamera, its sensor and lens coverage) and calibration, nothing when the camera is held, else the autocalibration's information at the result (refine_camera_and_rotations):

  • free_parameters — the names of the camera parameters refined;
  • parameters, sigma, covariance — their values, standard deviations and covariance (parameter units: pixels for f, cx, cy; the lens's own for its coefficients) — s² × VisualGeometryToolkitCore's implicit_covariance of the robust bundle (its Gauss–Newton (JᵀJ)⁻¹, each observation weighted by its loss's IRLS weight at the result, over the rotations, the track directions and the camera, the gauge frame removed), its camera block mapped from the camera chart to the parameters; s² = Σ wᵢ‖rᵢ‖² / ν, ν = 2n − p (n observations kept, p unknowns: 3(K − 1) rotations, 2 per kept track, the free parameters);
  • s2, nu — s² and ν;
  • reduced_eigenvalues_gauge_fixed — the eigenvalues (ascending) of the information reduced to the rotations and the camera (the inverse of their marginal covariance: the Schur complement of the directions), the gauge frame removed, its rows and columns scaled to a unit diagonal — positive when nothing but the gauge is free;
  • direction_min_eigenvalue — the least eigenvalue over the kept tracks of their 2 × 2 direction blocks (each positive: every kept direction observed).

PanoramaStitching.detect_features — Function
detect_features(camera::BoundedCamera, frame, descriptor; frame_map, tone_percentile, detector_margin_px, asift_tilts,
                max_detections, selection = :spread, detection_image = :tone, detection_noise = nothing,
                saturated = nothing) -> StructArray{DescribedFeature}

The features of one frame (H × W × 3 linear, or a VisualGeometryToolkitCore LinearRaw of mode :bin2; frame_map its map onto the camera's pixels, Core's raw_frame_map(L)) of camera, described by descriptor (a LoMaDescriptor) on the frame's tone sqrt(G / p) clamped to [0, 1] (p the tone_percentile-th percentile of the green channel inside the camera's imaged_region — its sensor ∩ its lens coverage ∩ its model's valid domain), the detections in the frame's pixels (the descriptor resamples the image to its network's input).

  • ASIFT (asift_tilts: a VisualGeometryToolkitFeatures tilt_schedule name, :imas25 or :untilted, or its (t, φ) pairs) on the detection_image: :tone, that tone's green at Features' default contrast threshold; :vst, the frame's variance-stabilized green cells (Features' variance_stabilize, noise σ = 1 at every level; the frame must be its LinearRaw) at detection_noise.noise_sigmas noise σ, detection_noise = (; electrons_per_dn, read_noise, noise_sigmas) the sensor's conversion gain and read noise (raw codes) at the frame's ISO.
  • No detection within three of its scales of a clipped pixel (Features' detection exclusion_mask): saturated, the frame's H × W saturation mask (a LinearRaw's own by default; nothing for an array frame: none).
  • The detections inside the imaged region less detector_margin_px at the lens coverage's edge (a camera whose coverage is its whole sensor: no margin).
  • selection: :spread (the one selection), about max_detections of the strongest spread over the frame (Features' spread_strongest, SSC, which the descriptor applies).

The result is the descriptor's own: VisualGeometryToolkitFeatures' described features (StructArray{DescribedFeature}), each a Detection in the frame's pixels (the detector's default: no keypoint covariance) and its DeDoDe description — the one feature store, in Features' canonical order of the detections (a function of the image: the same run to run, on any number of threads). frame_map takes a keypoint's centre to the camera's pixels (pair_bearings, the bundle of register_rotations).

PanoramaStitching.sample_frames — Function
sample_frames(camera::BoundedCamera, frames, rotations, directions; saturated, frame_map, rim_margin_px, fov_deg,
              vignetting, plane_maps = ntuple(_ -> frame_map, 3)) -> FrameSamples

The frames sampled at directions (unit 3-vectors of the common frame, any array): for every frame k and direction d, the ray f = R_kᵀ·d (rotations[k] with d = R_k·f), its exact sub-pixel sensor point u = π(f) through the calibrated camera (a VisualGeometryToolkitCore BoundedCamera, projected through its model's imaging_transform), the frame's value there by bilinear sampling (Core's image_sample!, no smoothing) and the vignetting model evaluated continuously there, vignetting(u, f) (a model defined on the sensor reads u, one defined on the ray's direction reads f; a calibration's vignetting, or (u, f) -> 1.0 for none). These are the observations of a vignetting validation (I_k / V_k must agree across the frames that see a direction: their spread is the model's error; score_seams scores them).

A frame sees a direction by render_panorama's masks: the ray within fov_deg of the camera frame's z axis, the sensor point inside the camera's imaged_region (its sensor ∩ its lens coverage ∩ its model's valid domain) less rim_margin_px at its lens coverage's edge, and at least 2 px inside the frame, and no saturated pixel among those any plane's value blends (the four at each plane's own sample position). frames are H × W × 3 linear images, saturated their H × W masks. frame_map is the frames' map onto the camera's pixels (VisualGeometryToolkitCore's raw_frame_map(L)), and plane_maps each plane's (bayer_plane_map(L, c); by default every plane's is the frame's): plane c is read at inv(plane_maps[c])(u).

Every step is a batch on the backend that owns frames and directions (Core's pointwise!, image_sample!: one kernel each on a GPU array, the same code on the host), so for device arrays vignetting must be kernel-lawful (isbits, no allocation, no throw); it is evaluated only where the frame sees the direction. The precision is the directions' element type.

PanoramaStitching.panorama_view — Function
panorama_view(projection, extent, deg_per_pixel; up = I) -> PanoramaView
panorama_view(camera, width, height; rotation = I) -> PanoramaView

The raster of a panorama and its pixel → ray map.

  • A cylindrical-family projection (Core's Equirectangular(), Cylindrical(), CylindricalEqualArea(), Mercator()): the raster spans extent = (lon₀, lon₁, lat₀, lat₁) degrees, deg_per_pixel along the longitude (its columns, from lon₀) and the same step in the projection's vertical coordinate (its rows, from lat₁ at the top: radians of latitude for the equirectangular, of tan φ, sin φ or asinh(tan φ) for the others), in the frame up (d = up·d_up, up_frame for a true-up panorama). Its pixel → ray map is LinearMap(up) ∘ inv(projection) ∘ raster, and its extent the raster's own (the latitudes of its row edges).
  • An azimuthal view, a virtual camera (a Core CameraModel): its width × height pixels, their rays LinearMap(rotation) ∘ Backprojection(camera) (d = rotation·f, f the virtual camera's ray). No new projection type: the lens chooses the view.

julia> cyl.size                                     # rows in steps of tan φ, columns of longitude

PanoramaStitching.render_panorama — Function
render_panorama(camera::BoundedCamera, frames, rotations, view; vignetting, frame_map, rim_margin_px, fov_deg,
                plane_maps = ntuple(_ -> frame_map, 3), gains = nothing) -> (; image, owner, coverage, extent)

The panorama of frames in the raster view (panorama_view) by the REVERSE warp, Core's image_warp/image_warp!: every pixel's ray d (the view's pixel → ray map) is rotated into each frame k (f = R_kᵀ·d, rotations[k] with d = R_k·f), projected through the calibrated camera (a VisualGeometryToolkitCore BoundedCamera, through its model's imaging_transform) to a sensor point between pixels, and sampled there bilinearly — one fused map camera ∘ R_kᵀ ∘ view.rays per frame — then divided by the vignetting vignetting(u, f), looked up at the sensor point u (camera pixels) and the ray f (the frame's camera frame) — a model defined on the sensor reads u, one defined on the ray's direction (a field angle) reads f; a Core AbstractVignetting reads u — and by the frame's exposure gain gains[k, c] of each colour plane c when gains is given (K × 3, per frame and plane: fit_vignetting's metadata.gains; nothing, the frames taken as one exposure). No seam, no blend, and each pixel keeps the value of the LAST frame that sees it, so a wrong vignetting shows as a step at every frame boundary.

A frame sees a pixel when its ray is within fov_deg of the camera frame's z axis, its sensor point lies inside the camera's imaged_region (its sensor ∩ its lens coverage ∩ its model's valid domain) less rim_margin_px at its lens coverage's edge, and at least 2 px inside the frame — the geometric masks of sample_frames, evaluated as batches at the warp's own sensor points. The render shows the frames as they are: a saturated pixel is rendered with its clipped value (a window that clips in every frame comes out bright, not empty); saturation masks measurements only (sample_frames, fit_vignetting, score_seams). frames are H × W × 3 linear images. frame_map is the frames' map onto the camera's pixels (VisualGeometryToolkitCore's raw_frame_map(L)), and plane_maps each plane's (bayer_plane_map(L, c); by default every plane's is the frame's); plane c is read through inv(plane_maps[c]) ∘ camera ∘ R_kᵀ ∘ rays, all three planes in one Core image_warp!. The masks and the vignetting are at the pixel centre's sensor point.

Every step runs on the backend that owns frames (GPU arrays: one kernel per warp, mask and update; the same code on the host); for device arrays vignetting must be kernel-lawful (it is evaluated only where a frame sees the pixel). The warp's math — the sensor points, the rays, the bilinear weights — is in Float64 whatever the frames' element type (a Float32 sensor coordinate of a 4000 px sensor is 2.4e-4 px coarse, enough to move a sampled value by 1e-4 relative); the image is in the frames' element type.

Returns image (rows × columns × 3, NaN where no frame sees the pixel), owner (the frame whose value a pixel holds, 0 for none), coverage (how many frames see each pixel), all on the frames' backend, and the view's extent.

The vignetting from the frames

PanoramaStitching.fit_vignetting — Function
fit_vignetting(vignetting, camera::BoundedCamera, frames, rotations; free_directions, gain_correction, saturated, frame_map,
               rim_margin_px, fov_deg, plane, plane_maps = ntuple(_ -> frame_map, 3), chart_spacing_deg, penalty_order, penalty_weight,
               vignetting_penalty_weight, solver, measurement_noise = IntensityNoise())
    -> FitResult{<:VisualGeometryToolkitCore.AbstractVignetting, VignettingMetadata}

The vignetting V and the exposure gains of a camera that only rotated, from its frames alone (no flat field): every pixel of every frame is the scene's radiance in its direction times V at its sensor point times the frame's gain, I_k(p) = g_k·L(R_k·f(p))·V(u(p)), and V is the factor the frames share where their views of the scene overlap (self-calibration). No interpolation of the frames: each value is read at its own pixel centre, its ray f(p) the camera's back-projection (Core's batched backproject of the model of the VisualGeometryToolkitCore BoundedCamera, computed once for the pixel grid the frames share), and the scene L is a B-spline over an AzimuthalChart of the covered sphere whose values are fitted with V and the gains.

  • vignetting: V's model and start, a VisualGeometryToolkitCore LogVignetting (V = exp(s), s a RadialVignetting or TensorProductVignetting B-spline of log V on the sensor, camera pixels; its control points the start of log V, e.g. zeros); held fixed, any vignetting the render takes (a Core model, or a callable vignetting(u, f): a calibration's map, a flat field; differentiable in u for IntensityAndPositionNoise). A sweep about one axis cannot observe a free 2-D V's tilt across that axis (a field exp(α·f_y) is unchanged by a pure yaw, so L absorbs it): use a tensor surface for frames with pitch and roll diversity.
  • free_directions: the matrix Z (n_control_points × m) V's control points move along, vec(P) = vec(P₀) + Z y (Core's restrict of V's ParameterManifold to Z): Core's pole_regular_basis(v) for a radial profile (smooth at its centre), the identity for a tensor surface, zeros(n, 0) to hold the given V fixed — then the fit is gain compensation under that V (a calibration's), the same system without V's block.
  • gain_correction: nothing (every frame at one exposure, g = 1), IntensityGains() (one gain per frame, `log Ik = log gk + log L(d)
    • log V(u),K − 1unknowns with the gaugeg₁ = 1`: L carries the first
    frame's exposure) or ChannelGains() (one per frame and colour plane, the gauge g_1c = 1: each other plane's gains solved under the fitted V, which stays one for all planes). In both modes of V — fitted, or held fixed. Use gains when the exposure or the light can change between frames: a fixed shutter under a drifting sky changes every frame's level, and the fit then puts the difference into V (on the rooftop sweep the gains reached +18 % and V at the domain's edge came out 7 % too dark without them); per channel when the white balance or the light's colour can change too. Required, no default (TOML: GAIN_CORRECTIONS).
  • The fit domain: every pixel of the H × W × 3 frames whose sensor point lies inside the camera's imaged_region (its sensor ∩ its lens coverage ∩ its model's valid domain) less rim_margin_px at its lens coverage's edge, and 2 px inside the frame, with its ray within fov_deg of the optical axis. A row (a pixel of a frame) counts when its saturated mask is false and its value positive. plane is the plane fitted (2: green), read at its own sample position. frame_map/plane_maps: the frames' and the planes' maps onto the camera's pixels (VisualGeometryToolkitCore's raw_frame_map, bayer_plane_map; by default every plane's is the frame's).
  • The model: log I = log g + log L(d) + log V(u), linear in log L's controls, log V's control points and the log gains: one weighted linear least squares (the weights normalized by their mean over the rows), with the difference penalty of order penalty_order (2 or 3) and weight penalty_weight (in units of one row of mean weight) on L's controls along both chart axes, chart_spacing_deg apart, a weak second-difference penalty on V's control points of weight vignetting_penalty_weight relative to the V block's mean diagonal (it fixes the control points the domain does not reach), and, with free directions, the gauge mean log V = 0 over the domain's pixels. A V held fixed enters as the numbers log V(u).
  • measurement_noise: the noise model of a row's measurement; the row weights are always the whitening of the log-I constraint by it (Core's whitening_factor). IntensityNoise (the default: noise in the value only, weight I², one solve) or IntensityAndPositionNoise (noise in the value and the pixel's sensor position: after the IntensityNoise solve, iterations more, each with its weights from the previous solve's model). TOML: MEASUREMENT_NOISES.
  • The solve: solver, a ConjugateGradient (Jacobi-preconditioned, on the frames' backend) or a SparseCholesky (the same system assembled sparse and factored on the host).

Every stage runs on the backend that owns frames (GPU arrays: KernelAbstractions kernels and atomic scatter-add; host arrays, the same kernels on the CPU). The saturated masks live on the same backend.

Returns a FitResult whose estimate is the fitted Core vignetting model (isbits: the vignetting keyword of render_panorama, sample_frames and score_seams, on the host or a device; the given model when V is held fixed), its termination record (VisualGeometryToolkitCore's retcode, a ReturnCode.T, and stop_rule; converged(result) its success) the worst over every linear solve of every plane, the other planes' of ChannelGains included (Core's worst_retcode: a CG that ended at its budget in any solve is MaxIters), iterations the CG's summed over every solve of every plane (0 for Cholesky), rms the rms of the log residual over n rows weighted by the last solve's weights, and VignettingMetadata (its gains, the frames' K × 3 g_kc: the gains keyword of score_seams and render_panorama; the residuals and the solves are the fitted plane's).

PanoramaStitching.AzimuthalChart — Type
AzimuthalChart(axis, half_extent, spacing)

The chart of the scene's log L: VisualGeometryToolkitCore's azimuthal-equidistant direction chart about axis (chart, Core's equidistant_chart(axis): a direction at angle θ from the axis lands at radius θ, radians, in the direction of its azimuth; singular only at -axis), and the uniform cubic tensor B-spline of n × n controls laid over the closed square area of it, [−half_extent, −half_extent + (n − 3)·spacing]² with n = ⌈2·half_extent / spacing⌉ + 4, so the controls are spacing (radians) apart: Core's spline_window / spline_value lattice, the controls [row, column] along the chart's (second, first) coordinate. fit_vignetting centres it so that its singular point is as far from the frames' coverage as the sphere allows (for a cap-like coverage, close to the mean optical axis; for a full 360° sweep, the pole the frames do not see).

PanoramaStitching.IntensityAndPositionNoise — Type
IntensityAndPositionNoise(; point_covariance, log_intensity_scale = 0.01, iterations = 1)

fit_vignetting's measurement_noise: noise in the pixel value and in the pixel's sensor position. A row is the implicit constraint

g(θ; x) = log I − log L(R_k·f(u)) − log V(u) − γ_k = 0

on the measurement x = (u, log I), the pixel's sensor point u (camera pixels) and its log value, of covariance Σ_x = blockdiag(Σ_u, σ²), and its weight is the frozen whitening of that constraint by this noise, 1/(G_x·Σ_x·G_xᵀ) (VisualGeometryToolkitCore's whitening_factor of the constraint's constraint_with_jacobians; in σ units), with the measurement gradient G_x = [−∇_u(log L∘R_k∘f + log V)ᵀ, 1] of the MODEL (its scene spline and V) at the previous solve; the first solve is IntensityNoise's.

  • point_covariance: Σ_u, VisualGeometryToolkitCore's CovMat{2} of a pixel's sensor point in camera pixels² (CovMat{2}(σ = σ_u) the isotropic σ_u²·I). A full or diagonal CovMat must be symmetric positive definite.
  • log_intensity_scale: σ, the standard deviation of log I (a constant floor: 0.01 is 1 % of the value).
  • iterations: the reweight-and-solve cycles after the IntensityNoise solve, 1 or 2 (the second re-evaluates G_x at the first's solution).

PanoramaStitching.score_seams — Function
score_seams(camera::BoundedCamera, frames, rotations, view; vignetting, saturated, frame_map, rim_margin_px, fov_deg, plane,
            plane_maps = ntuple(_ -> frame_map, 3), chunk, gains = nothing) -> (; ratio, rms, mad, n)

How well vignetting makes the frames agree where they overlap, at every pixel of the panorama raster view (panorama_view): the pixel's ray is sampled in every frame (sample_frames: the exact sub-pixel sensor point, the frame's bilinear value of plane, the vignetting there, the masks — saturated pixels EXCLUDED, a clipped value is no measurement) and, where at least two frames measure it, ratio is the median over the other frames of log(I_k/V_k) − log(I_last/V_last), last the frame the unblended render_panorama shows (the highest index). A correct V leaves only noise, misregistration and parallax; a wrong one leaves a step at every frame boundary. The score is the ratio's spread over the pixels measured twice or more: rms and the robust mad (StatsBase's mad, normalized: the 1.4826·MAD; misalignment and moving content inflate the rms), over n pixels. gains (optional, K × 3, per frame and colour plane: fit_vignetting's metadata.gains) divides each frame's exposure gain of plane out as well, log(I_k/(g_k,plane·V_k)); nothing takes the frames as one exposure.

The camera, the masks and the frames' and planes' maps (frame_map, plane_maps) are sample_frames's (camera a VisualGeometryToolkitCore BoundedCamera: its forward map and its imaged_region). The raster is sampled in chunks of chunk pixels (the samples are chunk × K × 3 values on the frames' backend: chunk to fit a shared GPU's memory). Returns ratio (rows × columns, NaN where fewer than two frames measure the pixel) on the frames' backend.
