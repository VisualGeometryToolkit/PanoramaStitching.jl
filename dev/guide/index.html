Getting started

PanoramaStitching turns a sweep of frames from a camera that only rotated into the rotation of every frame and a panorama. The camera is calibrated and fixed; its lens may have any field of view, so a fisheye imaging past 90° off axis works the same as a pinhole. All geometry is on unit bearings (VisualGeometryToolkitCore's backproject), never on the z = 1 plane, and every residual is measured in the camera's pixels.

The pipeline has four steps:

  1. register_rotations — the rotation R_k of every frame (d = R_k·f_k, a bearing f_k of frame k to a world direction d; the first frame's camera frame is the common frame).
  2. sample_frames — the frames sampled at directions spread uniformly over the covered sphere (fibonacci_directions): per direction, every frame that sees it with its sensor point, its value and the vignetting there — the observations of a vignetting validation or fit.
  3. render_panorama — the panorama into a view (panorama_view: a cylindrical-family projection or a virtual camera) by the reverse warp of the frames, unblended; render_cube_map renders the six faces of a cube map.
  4. fit_vignetting — the camera's vignetting from the frames alone (every pixel of every frame, at its own pixel centre, is the scene's radiance times V), and score_seams, how well a vignetting makes overlapping frames agree.

Steps 2 and 3 run on VisualGeometryToolkitCore's warping machinery — image_warp/image_warp!, image_sample! and pointwise!, KernelAbstractions kernels — on the backend that owns the frames: GPU arrays (CuArray) run on the device, host arrays run the same code on the CPU.

This page continues from the first example: its camera, circle, frames and reg are used below, and every block runs after it, in order, as the test suite runs them (the installation is the same).

The inputs

  • Frames: a Vector of H × W × 3 linear (not gamma-encoded) images in capture order. They may be binned relative to the calibration: raw_scale is the number of camera pixels per frame pixel.
  • Camera: a VisualGeometryToolkitCore camera. A parametric CameraModel serves every step. A grid model (CentralGenericModel) back-projects in the registration but has no closed-form forward map: supply its SplineProjection, fitted by VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation's fit_camera, as projection to the registration and as the camera of the sampling and the render. The example's camera as a 40 × 40 grid, with its forward map:
# Illustrative: VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation's fit_camera, one more package (pkg> add VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation)
using VisualGeometryToolkitCore: CentralGenericModel, SplineProjection, Rect, estimate
import VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation as VgtkEstimation

grid = CentralGenericModel(camera, (1000, 1000); grid_size = (40, 40))
projection = estimate(VgtkEstimation.fit_camera(SplineProjection, grid, Rect(0, 0, 1000, 1000) ∩ circle; tolerance = 1e-2))
# register_rotations(frames, grid; projection, …), then sample_frames(projection, …) and render_panorama(projection, …)
  • Image circle: circle, a Core Circle or Ellipse in camera pixels, the part of the sensor the lens illuminates, or WholePlane() for a lens that fills the sensor. Features and samples outside it are ignored.

1. Registering the rotations

register_rotations composes the stack's estimators and does no estimation of its own:

  1. Features (PanoramaStitching.detect_features): ASIFT keypoints inside the image circle, described by VisualGeometryToolkitFeatures' DeDoDe/LoMa descriptor (descriptor = :b), at most max_detections per frame. tone_percentile sets the exposure the detector sees; asift_tilts = [(1.0, 0.0)] is the untilted view only.
  2. Pairs: pairs = :all matches every frame pair; pairs = :lag_overlap only those at most max_lag apart in capture order, plus those whose averaged optical axes are within overlap_deg. Matches come from the LoMa matcher (matcher = :b128) and become bearing correspondences through the camera (PanoramaStitching.pair_bearings).
  3. Pairwise rotations: VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation's find_rotation (RANSAC on bearings, its sampler seeded from seed) verifies each pair; a pair with fewer than min_inliers inliers is dropped.
  4. Rotation averaging: the verified pairs form a ViewGraph; fit_rotation_averaging with averaging_loss gives every frame's rotation.
  5. Tracks: chain_tracks links the inlier matches into tracks across frames; inconsistent tracks (two keypoints of one frame) are dropped.
  6. Bundle: refine_rotations refines every rotation but the first, and one direction per track, minimizing the robust (bundle_loss) pixel reprojection error through projection with NLLSsolver.

The example's call states every setting: each is a keyword without a default, so a run states its whole configuration (say = println prints one line per frame, pair and step). The result, a Registration, holds the rotations, the view graph, per-pair statistics, the tracks and the bundle's result:

import VisualGeometryToolkitCore as VgtkCore

reg.pairs[(1, 2)]               # matches, inliers, rms_px (the inliers' rms Sampson distance) and rotation_deg
length(reg.tracks), reg.dropped # the tracks, and the inconsistent ones dropped
reg.bundle.rms                  # the bundle's rms reprojection error, pixels
reg.bundle.metadata.outcome     # how the bundle ended (RotationBundleMetadata)

On the example's sweep the rotations come back well within 0.05° and the bundle's rms is below a pixel.

2. Sampling the frames at directions

fibonacci_directions spreads directions uniformly over the sphere (the golden-angle spiral: equal area per direction, no pole bias), here restricted to those within fov_deg of some frame's axis. sample_frames takes each into every frame (f = R_kᵀ·d), projects it to its exact sub-pixel sensor point u through the camera, samples the frame there bilinearly and evaluates the vignetting model continuously at the same point, vignetting(u, f):

saturated = [falses(size(f, 1), size(f, 2)) for f in frames]     # mask clipped pixels here
D = fibonacci_directions(reg.rotations, 200_000; fov_deg = 89.0)
S = sample_frames(camera, frames, reg.rotations, D; saturated, circle, raw_scale = 2, rim_margin_px = 12.0,
                  fov_deg = 89.0, vignetting = (u, f) -> 1.0)

A frame sees a direction (S.seen[i, k]) when the ray is within fov_deg of the camera's z axis, its sensor point lies inside the image circle less rim_margin_px and 2 px inside the sensor, and no saturated pixel enters its value. For every direction seen by several frames, S.values[i, k, c] / S.vignetting[i, k] must agree across k: the spread is the vignetting model's error, and I_k = L(d)·V(u_k) is the equation of a panorama vignetting fit.

3. Rendering the panorama

panorama_view builds the raster and its pixel → ray map; render_panorama warps every frame into it (Core's image_warp, one fused map camera ∘ R_kᵀ ∘ rays per frame), divides by the vignetting and keeps, at each pixel, the LAST frame that sees it — no gain, no seam, no blend. It renders the frames as they are: a saturated pixel keeps its clipped value (the masks are for measurements — the sampling, the fit and the seam score — not for pictures):

cyl = panorama_view(VgtkCore.Cylindrical(), (-120.0, 120.0, -40.0, 40.0), 0.25)  # columns longitude, rows tan(latitude)
pano = render_panorama(camera, frames, reg.rotations, cyl; vignetting = (u, f) -> 1.0, circle, raw_scale = 2,
                       rim_margin_px = 12.0, fov_deg = 89.0)
pano.image      # rows × columns × 3, NaN where no frame sees the pixel
pano.owner      # the frame each pixel holds
pano.coverage   # how many frames see each pixel

The cylindrical family — Core's Equirectangular() (rows the latitude), Cylindrical() (tan φ), CylindricalEqualArea() (sin φ: every pixel subtends the same solid angle) and Mercator() (asinh(tan φ), conformal) — shares the longitude columns. An azimuthal view needs no projection of its own: it is a virtual camera, a Core CameraModel with the lens of the view, aimed by a rotation:

using VisualGeometryToolkitCore: Lens, Perspective, Stereographic
using StaticArrays: SMatrix
# a 1920 × 1080 rectilinear view, and a 'little planet': a stereographic view looking down
rect = panorama_view(CameraModel(1000.0px, SA[960.0px, 540.0px]; lens = Lens(Perspective())), 1920, 1080)
down = SMatrix{3, 3}(1.0, 0, 0, 0, 0, -1, 0, 1, 0)          # its axis along +y (down)
planet = panorama_view(CameraModel(400.0px, SA[1000.0px, 1000.0px]; lens = Lens(Stereographic())), 2000, 2000; rotation = down)

render_cube_map renders the six 90° faces and their horizontal cross. To level the horizon, supply up = up_frame(pitch, roll) to panorama_view (the first frame's pitch and roll against the true horizon, degrees).

4. The vignetting from the frames

A rotating camera sees each scene direction at several sensor points, so its frames determine its vignetting up to one scale. fit_vignetting uses every pixel of every frame inside the domain, read at its own pixel centre — no interpolation of the frames: log I_k(p) = log L(R_k·f(p)) + log V(u(p)), the pixel's ray f(p) the camera's back-projection (computed once: every frame shares the pixel grid), the scene log L a cubic B-spline over an AzimuthalChart of the covered sphere with a difference penalty, and log V a cubic B-spline in the distance from a centre (RadialVignetting) or over the sensor (TensorVignetting). The least squares is weighted by I²; its gauge is mean log V = 0 over the domain. With frame_gains = true every frame also has its own exposure gain (I_k = g_k·L·V, gauge g₁ = 1): a fixed exposure under changing light needs it, or V absorbs the frames' differences. The normal equations are accumulated by atomic scatter-add on the frames' backend (GPU arrays on the device) and solved by conjugate gradients there, or by a host Cholesky factorization:

space = RadialVignetting(SA[500.0, 500.0], 490.0, 16)          # centre, radius (camera pixels), controls
res = fit_vignetting(space, camera, frames, reg.rotations; saturated, circle, raw_scale = 2, rim_margin_px = 12.0,
                     fov_deg = 89.0, plane = 2, plane_offsets = nothing, chart_spacing_deg = 1.0, penalty_order = 3,
                     penalty_weight = 0.01, vignetting_penalty_weight = 1.0e-6, frame_gains = true,
                     solver = ConjugateGradient(; tolerance = 1.0e-9, max_iter = 40_000))   # or SparseCholesky()
V = VgtkCore.estimate(res)        # a SplineVignetting: V(u, f), the vignetting keyword of render_panorama
g = res.metadata.gains            # the frames' gains g_k (g₁ = 1): the gains keyword of score_seams and render_panorama

A sweep about one axis does not observe a 2-D V's tilt across it (a field that varies only with the ray's height is unchanged by a pure yaw, so L absorbs it): fit the tensor space only to frames with pitch and roll diversity. score_seams measures the result where it matters, at the seams of the unblended panorama: per raster pixel measured by two frames or more, the median log ratio of the other frames to the shown one (saturated pixels excluded), and its rms and 1.4826·MAD:

seams = score_seams(camera, frames, reg.rotations, cyl; vignetting = V, gains = g, saturated, circle, raw_scale = 2,
                    rim_margin_px = 12.0, fov_deg = 89.0, plane = 2, plane_offsets = nothing, chunk = 200_000)
seams.rms, seams.mad              # the spread of the log ratio over the n pixels two frames or more measure
corrected = render_panorama(camera, frames, reg.rotations, cyl; vignetting = V, gains = g, circle, raw_scale = 2,
                            rim_margin_px = 12.0, fov_deg = 89.0)   # the panorama with V and the gains divided out

The example's frames carry no vignetting and one exposure, so here V comes back close to 1 and every gain close to 1.
