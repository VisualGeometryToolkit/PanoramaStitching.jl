Getting started

PanoramaStitching turns a sweep of frames from a camera that only rotated into the rotation of every frame and an equirectangular panorama. The camera is calibrated and fixed; its lens may have any field of view, so a fisheye imaging past 90° off axis works the same as a pinhole. All geometry is on unit bearings (VisualGeometryToolkitCore's backproject), never on the z = 1 plane, and every residual is measured in the camera's pixels.

The pipeline has three steps, one function each:

  1. register_rotations — the rotation R_k of every frame (d = R_k·f_k, a bearing f_k of frame k to a world direction d; the first frame's camera frame is the common frame).
  2. sample_cells — a near-equal-area grid of world cells, sampled in every frame that sees it.
  3. render_panorama — the equirectangular image of per-frame cell values, composited in capture order.

Installation

using Pkg
Pkg.add("PanoramaStitching")

The inputs

  • Frames: a Vector of H × W × 3 linear (not gamma-encoded) images in capture order. They may be binned relative to the calibration: raw_scale is the number of camera pixels per frame pixel.
  • Camera: any VisualGeometryToolkitCore camera with project, backproject and backproject_with_jacobian. A fisheye is best modelled as a CentralGenericModel over a lens model:
using VisualGeometryToolkitCore: CameraModel, CentralGenericModel, KannalaBrandt, px
using StaticArrays: SA

camera = CentralGenericModel(CameraModel(300.0px, SA[500.0px, 500.0px]; lens = KannalaBrandt(-0.02)), (1000, 1000);
                             grid_size = (40, 40))
  • Image circle: circle = (cx, cy, r) in camera pixels, the part of the sensor the lens illuminates. Features and samples outside it are ignored.

1. Registering the rotations

register_rotations composes the stack's estimators and does no estimation of its own:

  1. Features (PanoramaStitching.detect_features): ASIFT keypoints inside the image circle, described by VisualGeometryToolkitFeatures' DeDoDe/LoMa descriptor (descriptor = :b), at most max_detections per frame. tone_percentile sets the exposure the detector sees; asift_tilts = [(1.0, 0.0)] is the untilted view only.
  2. Pairs: pairs = :all matches every frame pair; pairs = :lag_overlap only those at most max_lag apart in capture order, plus those whose averaged optical axes are within overlap_deg. Matches come from the LoMa matcher (matcher = :b128) and become bearing correspondences through the camera (PanoramaStitching.pair_bearings).
  3. Pairwise rotations: VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation's find_rotation (RANSAC on bearings, its sampler seeded from seed) verifies each pair; a pair with fewer than min_inliers inliers is dropped.
  4. Rotation averaging: the verified pairs form a ViewGraph; fit_rotation_averaging with averaging_loss gives every frame's rotation.
  5. Tracks: chain_tracks links the inlier matches into tracks across frames; inconsistent tracks (two keypoints of one frame) are dropped.
  6. Bundle: refine_rotations refines every rotation but the first, and one direction per track, minimizing the robust (bundle_loss) pixel reprojection error with NLLSsolver.
import VisualGeometryToolkitCore as VgtkCore

reg = register_rotations(frames, camera; raw_scale = 2, circle, tone_percentile = 99.5, detector_margin_px = 20.0,
                         asift_tilts = [(1.0, 0.0)], max_detections = 1024, descriptor = :b, matcher = :b128, min_score = 0.0,
                         pairs = :all, max_lag = 1, overlap_deg = 120.0, min_inliers = 40, seed = 1,
                         max_trials = 10_000, min_trials = 100,
                         averaging_loss = VgtkCore.HuberShape(), bundle_loss = VgtkCore.CauchyShape(),
                         bundle_scale_multiple = 3.0, bundle_iterations = 3, bundle_max_iter = 100,
                         bundle_ftol = 1e-10, bundle_xtol = 1e-10, say = println)

Every setting is a keyword without a default, so a run states its whole configuration. The result, a Registration, holds the rotations, the view graph, per-pair statistics (reg.pairs[(i, j)]: matches, inliers, the rms Sampson distance in pixels and the rotation angle), the tracks and the bundle's result (reg.bundle.rms is its rms reprojection error in pixels). On the package's synthetic fisheye sweep (four frames 35° apart) the rotations come back within 0.02° and the bundle's rms is below half a pixel.

2. Sampling world cells

world_cells divides the sphere into latitude bands of cell_deg degrees, each split into longitude cells of nearly equal area. sample_cells takes sub × sub jittered sample directions in every cell into each frame through the camera and averages the (slightly blurred) frame there:

using Random: Xoshiro

saturated = [falses(size(f, 1), size(f, 2)) for f in frames]     # mask clipped pixels here
samples = sample_cells(frames, saturated, reg.rotations, camera; raw_scale = 2, circle, cell_deg = 2.0, sub = 4,
                       blur_px = 1.0, rim_margin_px = 12.0, fov_deg = 89.0, field_angle = :lens, rng = Xoshiro(1))

A cell counts in a frame (samples.ok[k, i]) only when the frame sees all of it: its centre within fov_deg of the optical axis and every sample inside the image circle less rim_margin_px, unsaturated. The CellSamples also record each cell's field angle per frame (theta_model), which is what a vignetting fit downstream needs.

3. Rendering the panorama

render_panorama composites per-frame cell values in capture order — each cell takes the last frame that saw it — divided by that frame's gain (gains is K × 3, natural log; zeros for none):

K, N = size(samples.ok)
values = [samples.ok[k, i] ? samples.val[k, i, c] : NaN for k in 1:K, i in 1:N, c in 1:3]
image, extent, composite = render_panorama(samples.cells, values, samples.ok, zeros(K, 3))

image is the equirectangular raster (rows the elevation from the top, columns the azimuth, one pixel per cell, NaN where no frame saw the cell) and extent its (lon₀, lon₁, lat₀, lat₁) in degrees. Corrections of the frames themselves, such as dividing out a vignetting, are applied to values before rendering. To level the horizon, supply up = up_frame(pitch, roll) (the first frame's pitch and roll against the true horizon, degrees).
