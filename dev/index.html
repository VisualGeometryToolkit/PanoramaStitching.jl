PanoramaStitching

The panorama of a camera that only rotated (a sweep of a distant scene), seen through a calibrated lens of any field of view: the rotations of its frames (register_rotations — features, pairwise rotations, the view graph's rotation averaging, the tracks and the rotation and direction bundle), the frames sampled at directions of the sphere (sample_frames, the observations of a vignetting validation or fit), and the panorama by the reverse warp of the frames through the camera into any view — the cylindrical family, a virtual camera, a cube map — unblended (render_panorama, render_cube_map); the camera's vignetting from the frames alone, interpolation free (fit_vignetting), and the seams it leaves (score_seams).

Built on the VisualGeometryToolkit stack: VisualGeometryToolkitFeatures' ASIFT detector and LoMa descriptor and matcher, VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation's find_rotation and fit_rotation_averaging, VisualGeometryToolkitCore's cameras, rotation charts, panorama projections and warping machinery (image_warp, image_sample!, pointwise!: KernelAbstractions kernels on GPU arrays, the same code on the host); KernelAbstractions.jl and Atomix.jl (the vignetting fit's normal equations), Adapt.jl (a camera's forward map moved to the frames' backend), SparseArrays (the fit's host Cholesky solve), Graphs.jl, DataStructures.jl and NLLSsolver.jl.

Getting started walks through them on a fisheye sweep; the API page documents every function. The repository's examples/panorama_vignetting/ runs the whole chain on two RAW sweeps from configs of record.

PanoramaStitching is part of the VisualGeometryToolkit stack. Its one radiometric estimate is the vignetting a rotating camera's own frames determine (fit_vignetting, the scene and V fitted together, interpolation free); exposure, response and colour are left to the caller. MIT licence.
